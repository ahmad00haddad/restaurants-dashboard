3. Understanding of the assignment

Four people, four short films that show a real change in a working life: what they faced, what Skills4Work gave them, and what they do now. Each film should feel like the person's own story, not a report, while still showing what the donor needs to see: new skills, a job or business, income, and dignity for refugees and host community members alike.

4. Methodology

Pre-production (December). Briefing with the LI team on each beneficiary's background and results; a short call with each beneficiary to build trust and choose places and moments to film; one storyboard per story (opening image, interview questions, B-roll list, closing line, teaser idea) sent for LI approval before filming. Informed consent from every beneficiary; names, faces and locations protected on request, with particular care for refugees.

Filming (one full day per beneficiary). Interview in their own workplace with cinema camera, lav and boom sound and controlled interview lighting; B-roll of real work and daily life (hands, tools, customers, the road to work); vertical 9:16 shots framed on set, not only cropped later. All travel and logistics across governorates are on us.

Post-production (January – February). One 60–90 s 16:9 Full HD film and one 15–30 s 9:16 teaser per beneficiary; colour grading in DaVinci Resolve; sound clean-up, mix and licensed music; Arabic and/or English subtitles; LI, RDPP III and Denmark logos, acknowledgements and disclaimers per the visibility guidelines; up to three revision rounds on consolidated feedback. Delivery as Full HD masters, web-optimised MP4s, and complete raw footage.
