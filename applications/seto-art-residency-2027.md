## Artist Bio (≤250 words) — 196 words
Ahmad Haddad is a Jordanian cinematographer, film director and photographer based in Irbid. He has worked behind the camera since 2014. Over more than ten years he has served as director of photography, colorist and editor on short films, documentaries, brand campaigns and television productions. Clients include Al-Mamlaka TV in Jordan, Khutwa Media Production in Sharjah, and Graphite Studio, for whom he photographed Liwa Village in Abu Dhabi. He founded Faii House, a small production team in Jordan.

His personal work grows out of long journeys in harsh, quiet landscapes. He has shot in the deserts and coasts of Jordan, Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Tunisia, Mauritania and Morocco. One fifteen-day continuous shoot in the desert, with sunburn, little sleep, Bedouin tea, and sunrises and sunsets every day, shaped how he works today. He waits for light rather than forcing it. He builds trust with the people he films, and he lets daily rituals of work carry the story.

He works across digital cinema cameras, still photography and color grading. He is interested in how a place's labour, light and material shape the people who live there. He graduated from Yarmouk University.

## Why Salt Road, and what do you hope to gain from three weeks? (≤250 words) — 214 words
I grew up a short drive from the Dead Sea, the saltiest open water on earth. In Jordan, salt is not an ingredient. It is a landscape: white crusts on the shore, evaporation pans, workers whose skin and clothes carry the mineral. When I read that Salt Road Studio sits in Akō, a city whose identity was built on salt fields along the Seto Inland Sea, I felt an immediate connection between two places that have never been put side by side.

I want three weeks of slowness that commercial work never allows. On a client shoot I have hours. In Akō I would have time to return to the same shoreline at the same hour every day, to learn when the light changes on the water, and to earn the trust of people who still work with salt.

I also want to learn. Japan has a deep tradition of seeing, in its cinema, photography and craft. Being among other resident artists from different disciplines would push me out of the habits of a cinematographer. I hope to leave with a finished body of photographs, the first footage of a short film, and relationships in Akō that I can return to. Travelling to Japan to make work has been my goal for years. This residency would make it real.

## The project or area of exploration (≤250 words) — 223 words
Working title: **Two Salts**.

Two Salts is a photographic and film study that places Akō's salt heritage beside the salt of the Dead Sea in Jordan. I will not make a documentary about industry. I will make a portrait of how salt shapes time, light and labour in two coastal places on opposite sides of Asia.

In Akō I will:
1. Photograph the traditional salt-making sites and the Seto Inland Sea shoreline at fixed times each day, building a series of still images on light and evaporation.
2. Film the gestures of salt work and daily life in close detail: hands, tools, water, crystals. I will shoot on a digital cinema camera, and on 16mm if the studio allows.
3. Record ambient sound of the sea, the wind and the work, to use as the soundtrack.
4. With permission, make quiet portraits of residents connected to salt, the sea or Akō's history.

Before arriving, I will shoot the matching Jordan half at the Dead Sea, so in Akō I can respond to it image by image. At the end of the residency I will share a sequence of prints and a rough cut of a 5–8 minute short film. After returning, I will finish the film and submit it to festivals, crediting Salt Road Studio.
